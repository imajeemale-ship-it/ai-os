## Three layers: choose, make, distribute

**Idea selection and research (before recording)**
- **01:40 Visual anchors / napkin test:** A video concept needs something that must be *shown* to be understood; if a friend would need to see a phone image or drawing, the idea has a visual anchor. Creator additionally recommends Reddit as a source of niche-specific striking visuals. M3: save gameplay incidents and visual proof as idea seeds; reject purely verbal premises with no compelling gameplay to show.
- **21:57 Adapt outliers:** Benchmark a video's performance against its *own channel's normal views*, not absolute views. Study outliers across genres, and repeat M3's own breakout concepts with fresh stakes. MrBeast example: a comparison format succeeded across multiple niches, so he adapted it; not evidence that every adaptation will work.
- **27:44 Copy with taste / 29:42 3% change:** Adapt underlying format, not exact script, voice, cuts or timing. Find cross-genre inspiration. M3: translate a high-performing real-world challenge into a GTA-specific premise with Rico's own style and skills.
- **32:43 Solve popular problems:** Creator recommends searching YouTube autocomplete in incognito, reviewing questions on Quora, and polling audiences to find demand. M3: prioritize audience questions and recurring GTA frustrations as potential episodes, not only clips the team already happens to have.
- **34:45 Interested is interesting:** Authentic enthusiasm is an asset; spontaneous reactions and real curiosity can carry a video. Preserve Rico's real reactions instead of cutting every pause or forcing manufactured energy.

**Story, footage and editing (production)**
- **06:35 R.A.I.N.Y. intro — FULL acronym:** **R**esult up front; **A**ddress the objection (viewer skepticism); **I**nstant (creator's long-form rule of thumb: roughly 45 seconds or less); **N**ow (why timely); **Y**ou (why this creator can deliver). These are *five checks*, not five mandatory scripted lines. For GTA: tease the challenge/result, show credible proof, enter action quickly, explain relevance when genuine, and establish Rico's unique authority/voice. Never spoil a genuinely uncertain outcome by inventing one.
- **18:07 First frame (Shorts):** Frame zero should make the premise legible without an extended monologue. Physical props were an example, not a requirement. M3: use an immediately comprehensible GTA image/action and readable caption.
- **36:26 Avoid eye fatigue:** One clear focal point at a time; dim/zoom surrounding details when explaining something complex. Shorts need captions in safe visual zones, away from platform UI. MVI should check overlays/facecam/text collisions and flag candidates for *human* review; a frame sample alone cannot prove sustained fatigue.
- **38:05 Production paradox:** For small channels, clear sound, deliberate framing and preparation matter more because viewers don't yet trust the creator. Apparent improvisation often rests on preparation; cited examples include scripted comedy and prepared streams. M3: plan challenge beats and audio capture before recording; preserve authentic moments in edit.
- **40:42 Three B's:** **Basic, Bold, Brief** — simplify jargon, use direct language and remove redundant descriptions. Creator's heuristics: ~25 words or fewer per sentence and no more than three sentences per paragraph. These are editing aids, not mandatory constraints on Rico's natural speech.
- **42:05 Off-camera craft:** Build uncommon skill outside the camera, then feature it. M3's differentiator is Rico's deep GTA knowledge and execution, not just effects or thumbnails. Let his expertise produce story ideas and surprising gameplay outcomes.

**Packaging, iteration and business**
- **12:59 Five C's of thumbnails:** **Composition, Context, Clean, Curiosity, Color.** Creator proposes scaling to **18%** to inspect mobile legibility and aiming for **three elements or fewer** as simplifying heuristics. Distinguish genuine curiosity from misleading imagery.
- **16:32 Macro then micro:** Test substantially different thumbnail concepts first (camera angle, composition, subject); then refine the winner. Creator cites YouTube's watch-time-based thumbnail experiments and suggests at least two rounds where feasible. No A/B conclusions without actual analytics.
- **47:01 Earn the right to rebel:** Brand creative freedom is earned through demonstrated reliable work and relationships. M3: build a portfolio of completed, well-executed videos before expecting sponsors to approve unconventional concepts. Not an immediate editing feature.
- **50:13 Be more like Mozart:** Repetition and deadlines create learning; perfectionism can prevent publishing. Casey Neistat example contrasts daily uploads with slower output later. **Important tension with M3 quality-first:** use repeatable practice and a clear *quality gate*, not indiscriminate posting. Each published video should test one or two hypotheses and generate learning.

## Priority implementation, not a generic checklist
1. **Pre-edit creative brief**: premise, visible proof, viewer promise, objection, Rico's distinctive angle, likely payoff, footage available, source references.
2. **Evidence-led rough-cut review**: segment/scene timeline + audiovisual observations + timestamp-specific suggestions, uncertainty labels, audio/dialogue analysis.
3. **Packaging brief**: three genuinely different thumbnail/title hypotheses; Five C's; first-frame guidance for Shorts.
4. **Post-release learning**: store baseline, CTR, retention graph, thumbnail experiments, comments, and whether predictions matched performance; requires real YouTube Analytics permissions.
5. **Human calibration**: David marks AI suggestions useful/not useful and why; compare against his actual edits before automating scores.
