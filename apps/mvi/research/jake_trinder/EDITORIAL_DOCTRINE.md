## Thesis

A video's success is driven by the quality of a series of context-dependent decisions rather than editing polish alone. Evaluate an edit by whether it satisfies expectations established for the right viewer. Never treat flashier, faster, or busier as automatically better. This is a *coaching lens*, not an objective universal formula or a forecast.

## M3 gaming adaptation — our inference, not verbatim Jake rules

For GTA gameplay and comedic promos:
- Viewer promise may be: a wild encounter, impossible escape, embarrassing failure, surprising skill, a funny team dynamic.
- A practical storytelling sequence may be: setup -> stakes -> escalation -> reaction -> payoff.
- Within the first seconds, check whether viewers can recognize the premise. No fixed mandatory cut count or effect interval.
- Do not delete quiet setup if it supplies necessary tension before the joke.
- MVI should suggest e.g. 'move verified reaction at 00:28 ahead of unclear setup' **only if** the audiovisual analysis found and reviewed the actual reaction.
- Without verified evidence, ask an editor to inspect a range; label suggestions as hypotheses.

## Desired MVI coaching output

1. Intended viewer, style, promised experience, desired emotion/payoff; explicitly list unknowns.
2. Strongest evidence-backed insight (timestamps and actual observable content).
3. At most three prioritized edits: where, issue, why the viewer would care, exact proposed action, evidence, confidence.
4. Promise acceptance criteria: passed / failed / unknown. Unverified must remain unknown.
5. Preserve list: things *not* to change, including intentional pauses or raw delivery if working.
6. Post-publish analytics interpretation only when actual views, CTR, retention and qualitative comments are available.
7. Short follow-up test: one revised cut or A/B packaging experiment; compare results against relevant baseline.

Never fabricate dialogue, humor, plot, creative scores, audience reaction or detailed visual events based on scene boundaries, technical metadata, sampled frames alone, or unverified marketing claims.
